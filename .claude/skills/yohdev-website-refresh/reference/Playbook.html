01 · positioning

Who YohDev
is for.

YohDev is a remote-first development partner for teams who are tired of managing agencies. The brand's core promise is seniority without overhead — a team that ships without needing to be hand-held.

Positioning statement: YohDev is the development partner for SMB founders and product teams who need senior engineering execution without the overhead of hiring, onboarding, or managing a traditional agency queue.

What makes YohDev different

  • Senior-first, not team-paddedYou work directly with experienced engineers — not an account manager who relays messages to a junior dev.
  • Scope-honestHonest written roadmaps before work starts. No black-box phases, no surprise invoices at the end of a sprint.
  • Remote-nativeNot remote-adapted. Built from the ground up for async communication, clear documentation, and weekly demos.
  • Service breadth without agency bloatFrom WordPress block themes to React apps, AI readiness, and legacy modernization — one partner, not a roster of specialists to coordinate.

02 · audience

Who the
site is for.

The homepage has one job: convert a founder or technical lead who found YohDev through a referral or search and needs to quickly answer "are these the right people for my project?"

Primary audience

SMB Founders (5–50 person companies)

  • Need development work done but can't justify a full-time hire
  • Have been burned by agencies that overpromise and under-deliver
  • Decide fast when they trust the provider
  • Mobile is important but desktop is where they research vendors

Product / Marketing Leads at Growth Teams

  • Have a roadmap item that their internal team can't get to
  • Need a clear SOW and predictable timeline
  • Will share the site with a decision-maker before reaching out
  • Skim quickly — proof and clarity beat cleverness

Secondary audiences

  • Referral recipientsSomeone was told "you should talk to YohDev" — arrives with high intent, just needs to confirm legitimacy quickly.
  • Potential collaborators / subcontract partnersOther agencies or freelancers scoping white-label capacity.

Who this is NOT for

  • Enterprise procurement teams (too slow, too committee-driven)
  • Clients who want the cheapest option
  • Clients who need a full design agency (brand, print, campaigns)
  • Solo micro-businesses with a $500 budget

What they're checking for on the homepage

  • Legitimacy"Are these real engineers or a template shop?"
  • Clarity of scope"Do I understand what they actually do?"
  • Proof"Have they done this before for someone like me?"
  • Access"Is there an easy way to start a conversation?"

04 · voice & tone

How YohDev
sounds.

The YohDev voice is confident without being cocky, technical without being cold. It reads like a senior engineer talking to a peer — direct, specific, no filler.

Is

  • Direct and specific
  • Technically credible
  • Honest about tradeoffs
  • Warm but not performatively friendly
  • Irreverent where earned

Is Not

  • Corporate or buzzword-heavy
  • Over-promising ("best in class")
  • Cutesy or emoji-forward
  • Vague or abstract ("solutions," "journey")
  • Loud for its own sake

In their voice

"The development partner you don't have to manage."

Not their voice

"We offer comprehensive digital solutions to accelerate your brand's journey toward transformation."

Words to use / avoid

Use

  • Partner, not vendor
  • Ship, build, deploy
  • Senior, experienced
  • Honest, direct, clear
  • Weekly demos, written plans

Avoid

  • Solutions, synergy, leverage
  • Best-in-class, world-class
  • Journey, transformation
  • Cutting-edge, innovative
  • Cheap, affordable (price signal OK, not this framing)

Design principles

  • Dark canvas, not dark modeThe dark aesthetic is the identity, not a preference toggle. It signals technical depth and avoids the bright-white generic agency look.
  • Teal as the only accentUsed for CTAs, code labels, highlights, and interactive states. Never used decoratively for its own sake.
  • Mono code labels everywhereJetBrains Mono eyebrows (// services, phase_01) create a developer-native visual language that differentiates from general creative agencies.
  • No gradients, no blobs, no stock photos of laptopsThe faceted SVG art in the hero is the only decorative element. All other visuals are structural.
06 · homepage

Section by
section rationale.

Every section earns its place by answering one question the primary persona has when they arrive. The flow follows a trust-building sequence, not a traditional agency template.

  • Hero — "What do you do and is it for me?" Headline: "The development partner you don't have to manage." Direct, specific, addresses the primary pain (managing agencies) in the first five words. CTA is double: primary action (Start a project) and secondary exploration (See what we build) for people not ready to commit.
  • Capabilities marquee — "What's in scope?" Scrolling ticker of capabilities establishes breadth without a long bulleted list. Keeps the page moving and answers "what kind of work" before the services section.
  • Services accordion — "Can they do the specific thing I need?" Seven service lines, each with a one-line description that is technically specific. The accordion format lets the visitor self-select without scrolling a wall of text. Services are named by deliverable, not by team function.
  • Process — "How does this actually work?" Four steps (Discovery → Roadmap → Agile Sprints → Optimize) answer "what does working with YohDev look like?" Placed after services because visitors need to know what you do before caring how you do it.
  • CTA — "How do I start?" Final section is minimal: one headline, one subline, one email address. No form, no calendar embed. Direct email contact signals that a human will respond — not a CRM drip sequence.

Strategic decisions

No pricing on the homepage

YohDev's engagements are scoped per project. Showing pricing would either over-promise (too low) or filter out qualified leads (too high). The CTA copy sets expectations: "honest feedback on scope, timeline, and whether we're the right fit."

No hero video or animation

The audience is senior technical leads and founders on fast connections — but the principle holds: video in a hero signals "marketing-heavy agency," which is the anti-positioning for YohDev. The faceted SVG art is lightweight and developer-native.

Single primary CTA color

Teal is only used on one CTA per screen. Secondary CTAs are ghost/outline. This prevents button soup and keeps the accent meaningful — clicking the teal button is always the most important action on the page.

Email address visible, not hidden behind a form

Showing hello@yohdev.com directly signals directness and accessibility — core to the brand voice. Founders who've been burned by agencies appreciate knowing there's a real person on the other end.

07 · messaging

The three
things we say.

Every piece of copy on the homepage is downstream of three messaging pillars. If a section doesn't serve one of these, it doesn't exist.

  • Pillar 1 — Senior execution, not agency overhead The homepage must communicate that YohDev's work is done by senior engineers who own their output — not routed through account managers or delegated to juniors. This is the primary differentiator and the first thing the hero communicates.
  • Pillar 2 — Scope honesty and process clarity Founders who've been burned by agencies care deeply about predictability. The process section and CTA copy both reinforce that YohDev writes scopes before it starts, ships weekly demos, and tells you honestly if it's not the right fit.
  • Pillar 3 — Breadth without bloat YohDev handles the full range of web development work a growing company needs — but doesn't hire armies of specialists to do it. The services section proves breadth; the "partner, not vendor" framing counters the "too big to know our name" fear.

Messaging guardrails

  • Never claim "best" or "fastest"The audience has heard these claims before. Specific language ("weekly demos," "written plan before work starts") is more persuasive than superlatives.
  • No case study carousels on the homepageCarousels hide content and reduce trust with technical audiences. Proof is delivered through specificity of service descriptions, not a slider.
  • Don't describe the process as "seamless" or "effortless"These words signal that the copy is written by someone who hasn't done the work. YohDev's audience will notice.
